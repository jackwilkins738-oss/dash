# Monopoly lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **A genuinely small market to start in.** "Ticket-to-invoice for 5–20 truck roll-off operators" is narrow enough that a big share is conceivable. The Monopoly lens likes a small market you can win before you expand, and this is one.
- **A specific, money-shaped pain.** Unbilled overages are lost revenue the owner can count, not a vague efficiency gain. If the founder's account holds, the product can pay for itself in a month, and that makes a narrow wedge believable.
- **Cheap to test.** Under £50/month to run, and 20 conversations plus a £99 pilot before serious code. Being wrong costs little.
- **A data asset could build up.** Every photographed ticket teaches the system each disposal site's ticket layout and fee schedule. In theory that is proprietary know-how that compounds. Today it is only potential.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Distribution.** SEO and operator communities, with no outbound or personal selling, is the weakest possible route to owners of small trucking firms. Many of them are not searching for software and do not hang out in online communities. Nothing in the brief says how the first 100 customers arrive or what each costs, and at £79–£149/month the acquisition cost has to be very low. Under this lens, a product with no way to reach buyers is not a business.
2. **No secret, no 10x, easy to copy.** Photo-to-OCR-to-invoice is an incremental improvement built on commodity parts (off-the-shelf OCR/AI, Stripe). Existing hauling, dumpster-rental and field-service software could add "photograph the scale ticket" as a feature, and a funded competitor could match the MVP within weeks. Nothing in the brief answers "what stops a copycat in month six?" or "will this hold its position in ten years?"
3. **Team and geography unknown.** No founder background, no industry contacts, no target country. Pricing is in GBP but the vocabulary ("roll-off") and the 5–20 truck segment may describe a different market. If the founder does not know which country's operators they serve, they cannot yet own a small market there.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which country, and roughly how many 5–20 truck roll-off firms are in it? (Unknown. This sets the ceiling on "a big share of a small market".)
- Who already sells software to these operators, and does any of it capture scale tickets today? (Competitors not supplied. Name them before building.)
- What do you know about this industry that others do not? What is the secret?
- Why now? What has changed (OCR accuracy, operator adoption of phones, disposal-site digitisation) that makes this possible or needed today?
- How do the first 25 paying customers find you, and what does each one cost to win? Why rule out outbound?
- How much overage actually goes unbilled per truck per month? Is that measured or anecdotal?
- If an incumbent ships this feature next quarter, what do you have that they don't?
- Could the ticket data become a network effect (shared site fee tables, disposal-site partnerships), or will it stay a per-customer convenience?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Pick the market.** Name the target country and an estimated count of target operators before the pilot.
2. **Map the competition.** A written map of existing tools these operators use, with an honest answer to whether any already does ticket capture.
3. **Prove distribution, not just demand.** At least 5 of the 20 conversations convert to paid £99 pilots, with a recorded source and cost for each. Open to outbound if SEO alone does not produce them.
4. **Quantify the pain.** Pilot data showing unbilled overage recovered per customer is clearly above the subscription price.
5. **State a durability plan.** One credible route to a moat within 24 months: a site-level ticket and fee dataset, disposal-site integrations, or a category-owning brand in one region.
