# Offers lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The pain can be counted in money.** Overages that never get billed are lost cash, not just a nuisance. An offer that says "we find money you are already owed" is the best kind of dream outcome, because the buyer can check it on their own invoices.
- **Quick result.** One photo, one flag, one payment link. If it works, the owner sees value on the first job, not after a long setup project.
- **Small, sensible MVP.** The scope sticks to the reconciliation problem and leaves out a full dispatch suite.
- **Testing before building.** Twenty conversations and a paid £99 pilot are the right order of work.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The market is hard to reach, and the plan rules out the one channel that tends to work.** Owners of 5–20 truck fleets are busy, often not searching online for software, and get through trade relationships and phone calls. Relying only on "SEO and communities, no outbound" puts the riskiest part of the business, reaching buyers, on the weakest channel. The geography is also unclear. "Roll-off" is the US term, the prices are in GBP, and the UK trade mostly says "skip hire". The founder may not yet know which market they are in, and SEO cannot target a market that has not been defined.
2. **Too much effort is asked of the people who get no benefit.** The owner gets the outcome, but the drivers have to change their habits by photographing every ticket, every time. If drivers skip it, the overage capture fails quietly and the main promise breaks. Whether OCR can read faded, wet or crumpled tickets from many different weighbridges is also unproven, so the buyer has no reason yet to believe it will work.
3. **The pitch is a cheaper app, not money recovered.** At £79–£149 a month with no guarantee and no proof, this looks like a commodity tool next to whatever hauling or skip-hire software owners already use (competitors are an open question). If the recovered overages are as large as the founder believes, the offer is underpriced and badly framed. If they are not, there is no offer.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which country, and what do operators there call themselves and search for?
- In your 20 conversations, how much in unbilled overages does a typical operator say they lose each month? Can any of them show you on paper?
- How many operators paid the £99 pilot, compared with how many said "sounds good"?
- What do these operators use today, and does it already handle scale tickets?
- What happens when a driver forgets the photo? How does the owner find out?
- How do the first 10 customers find you if you will not call anyone?
- Could you guarantee "we recover at least the cost of the subscription in month one, or it's free"? Could you charge double and still be the obvious choice?
- What is your link to this industry? (Not supplied.)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Pick the market** (country and the trade's own terms) before building anything.
2. **Get proof:** at least 5 of the 20 operators pay for the pilot, and at least 3 of them document real unbilled overages on their past tickets.
3. **Reframe and guarantee the offer:** sell it as recovered revenue, with a guarantee that the subscription pays for itself or the operator does not pay, and price it against the money recovered, not against other apps.
4. **Lower the drivers' effort:** show drivers capturing tickets on a real fleet for 2 weeks with a high completion rate, plus an OCR accuracy figure measured on real tickets.
5. **Allow direct outreach to the first cohort:** a few phone calls or yard visits to get the first customers, even if the long-term plan is self-serve.
